Overview

As pretraining increasingly repeats data, every run faces three questions: how many epochs to take, how that number should change with model size, and whether anything besides the epoch count matters.

We answer them by pricing a repeated token against two references at the same model size. Against fresh data at equal compute, the cost of repetition follows a single variable: the number of extra epochs divided by the unique tokens per parameter. Against the same data seen once, a second epoch is worth nearly as much as a fresh one, and repeated tokens fall to half the value of fresh ones after a critical epoch count that grows with the training budget per parameter but hardly with model size.

The same variable explains size trends that appear to conflict in earlier work. Larger models tolerate fewer epochs when the corpus is fixed, but not when unique data grow with the model. And counts alone do not determine loss: the order, allocation and source of the repeats all change the result.

Two prices

Every repeated run of R epochs over U unique tokens, D = RU in total, is compared with two one-epoch runs of the same model.

Value · data-matched η = (Ueq − U) / (D − U)

Baseline: one epoch on the same U tokens. Ueq is the fresh budget that reaches the same loss, so η is the fresh-token equivalent of each repeated token: 1 if repeats were as good as fresh data, 0 if worthless.

Cost · compute-matched ΔL = L(N, U, R) − L(N, D, 1)

Baseline: one epoch on D fresh tokens at the same compute and schedule. ΔL is the extra held-out loss, in bits per byte, paid for repeating instead of collecting new data.

Fitted laws

Cost of repetition Eq. 3 · §4.2

ΔL = 0.0203 · z1.07

z = (R − 1) Ntotal / U: extra epochs divided by unique tokens per parameter. Fitted to the 113 of 140 repeated runs above the 0.002 bpb resolution, R² = 0.96; no further size term is needed once embeddings are counted.

Critical epochs Eq. 4 · §4.3

Rc ≈ 2.7 · (D/N)0.24

The epoch count at which a repeated token is worth half a fresh one (η = ½). Fits 35 measured crossings to 8% RMS: 4.0–5.3 epochs up to 20 tokens per parameter, 6.9–8.3 at 100, 10.0–11.8 at 300.

Allocation at fixed U Eq. 5 · §4.4

Nopt = √(C/6τ), Ropt = √(τC/6) / U

With unique data fixed, compute goes to model size and epochs in roughly equal shares (fitted N ∝ C0.52–0.53, R ∝ C0.47–0.48), holding τ = D/N at 16–24, until loss stops improving near Rc.

Best epochs at fixed U App. K

R* ≈ 4.5 · (U/N)0.51

Loss-minimizing epoch count in the fixed-corpus sweeps from 14M to 2B (R²log = 0.94). Because it depends on U/N, a fixed corpus shifts the optimum to fewer epochs as models grow.

Range of validity. Eqs. 3 and 4 are fitted on CommonCrawl with GPT-2 byte-level BPE, 23M–361M non-embedding parameters, 5–300 training tokens per parameter and 2–16 epochs, without weight decay or dropout. The constants may shift under other data or regularization.

    Beyond counts

    At identical token counts, how the repeats are laid out still changes loss.

    Order
    +0.46 bpb
    Replaying shards consecutively instead of spacing them across epochs, at 511M and 16 epochs. Reshuffling every epoch changes loss by less than 0.01.
    Allocation
    +0.0039 bpb
    Concentrating repeats on fewer samples, a third of the data four times versus half twice; 95% CI [+0.0033, +0.0045], positive in all 18 paired differences.
    Source
    r = −0.89
    Correlation between a source’s token entropy and its loss rise at 16 epochs. GitHub code rises 0.16 bpb above its minimum; Books does not rise.
    Re-tokenization
    −0.154 bpb
    BPE-dropout (p = 0.05) on repeats at 16 epochs. It costs 0.021 bpb at one epoch and 0.010 at four, so it pays only under heavy repetition.

    Partial repetition follows a different law. Drawing a fraction f of the budget from a repeated subset costs ΔL ∝ f1.63 zsub0.53 (R² = 0.89); Eq. 3 scaled by f overstates it about fourfold, so it serves as a conservative bound.

    In practice

    • Transfer epoch counts with data, not size.With unique data fixed, larger models should take fewer epochs; when unique data grow with the model, the best epoch count barely changes. To reuse an epoch count on a larger model, scale the unique data with it.
    • Larger budgets per parameter tolerate more repetition.Repeats fall to half the value of fresh tokens after about four to five epochs up to 20 tokens per parameter, and about ten at 300.
    • Grow model and epochs together, up to Rc.With unique data fixed, extra compute goes to both in roughly equal shares until loss stops improving near the critical epoch count. Ignoring the cost of repetition loses at most 0.002 bpb up to that point.
    • Spread and shuffle repeats.Space repeats across epochs and samples, repeat lower-entropy sources less, and re-tokenize only under heavy repetition. Compare such choices at separately annealed horizons; intermediate checkpoints can misrank them.

    Setup

    Grid
    Five sizes from 23M to 361M non-embedding parameters; 5–300 tokens per parameter; 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 epochs with U = D/R
    Sweeps
    Fixed corpus (14M–2B, up to 32 epochs), U = 2.5N (23M–1.2B), D = 20N partial repetition, order, source entropy and re-tokenization studies
    Models
    Decoder-only Transformers with grouped-query attention, RoPE, ReLU² MLPs and tied embeddings; context 1,024
    Optimizer
    Muon for hidden matrices, Adam elsewhere; no weight decay, dropout or clipping; learning rate annealed to each run’s horizon
    Data
    SlimPajama: CommonCrawl, C4, GitHub, Books, arXiv, Wikipedia and StackExchange; GPT-2 byte-level BPE
    Metric
    Bits per byte on held-out test sets of each source; paired studies use three or more replicates
    Compute
    Eight H800 GPUs

    Limitations: the models are far smaller than frontier systems, and since weight decay lowers the cost of repetition, the constants of Eqs. 3 and 4 may shift under other regularization.
