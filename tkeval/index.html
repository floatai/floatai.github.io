Tokenization · Findings of EMNLP 2024

Tokenization falling short

On subword robustness in large language models

Yekun Chai, Yewei Fang, Qiwei Peng, Xuhong Li

Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2024, pages 1582–1599

Paper arXiv Code Dataset

Overview

Language models tokenize raw text into subword identifiers from a fixed vocabulary. This process is sensitive to typographical errors and length variation, and largely blind to the internal structure of tokens. We call these issues the curse of tokenization and measure how much they still affect LLMs.

Research questions

  • Complex problem solvingTasks that need character access, such as cycled letters in a word, word unscrambling and identifying math theorems.
  • Token structure probingWhat models know about the characters and sub-units inside their own tokens.
  • Typographical variationRobustness to character-level and token-level perturbations of the input.

Findings

  • Scale helps, partly.Scaling model parameters mitigates the effects of tokenization.
  • Typos still bias models.LLMs still suffer from biases induced by typos and other text format variations.
  • Subword regularization helps.Training with subword regularization such as BPE-dropout mitigates the issue.
