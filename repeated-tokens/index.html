What is a repeated token worth?

Public text grows more slowly than compute, so pretraining increasingly reuses data. Training models from 14M to 2B parameters on repeated tokens, we find that a second epoch is worth almost as much as fresh data, that the cost of repetition follows a single variable, and that repeats fall to half the value of fresh tokens after a critical epoch count set by the training budget per parameter, and hardly at all by model size.

As pretraining increasingly repeats data, every run faces three questions: how many epochs to take, how that number should change with model size, and whether anything besides the epoch count matters.

We answer them by pricing a repeated token against two references at the same model size. Against fresh data at equal compute, the cost of repetition follows a single variable: the number of extra epochs divided by the unique tokens per parameter. Against the same data seen once, a second epoch is worth nearly as much as a fresh one, and repeated tokens fall to half the value of fresh ones after a critical epoch count that grows with the training budget per parameter but hardly with model size.

The same variable explains size trends that appear to conflict in earlier work. Larger models tolerate fewer epochs when the corpus is fixed, but not when unique data grow with the model. And counts alone do not determine loss: the order, allocation and source of the repeats all change the result.

Two ways to price a repeated token

Every repeated run of R epochs over U unique tokens, D = RU in total, is compared with two one-epoch runs of the same model.

Value, data-matched. The baseline is one epoch on the same U tokens. If Ueq is the fresh budget that reaches the same loss, then

η = (Ueq − U) / (D − U)(1)

is the fresh-token equivalent of each repeated token: 1 if repeats were as good as fresh data, 0 if they were worthless.

Cost, compute-matched. The baseline is one epoch on D fresh tokens at the same compute and schedule. The price of repeating instead of collecting new data is the extra held-out loss, in bits per byte:

ΔL = L(N, U, R) − L(N, D, 1)(2)

One variable sets the cost; tokens per parameter set the limit

Define the load z = (R − 1) Ntotal / U: extra epochs divided by unique tokens per parameter. Across the grid, the cost of repetition is a power law in z alone:

ΔL = 0.0203 · z1.07(3)

It fits the 113 of 140 repeated runs above the 0.002 bpb resolution with R² = 0.96, and needs no further size term once embeddings are counted.1

The value of a repeat crosses one half, η = ½, at a critical epoch count that depends on tokens per parameter D/N:

Rc ≈ 2.7 · (D/N)0.24(4)

It fits 35 measured crossings to 8% RMS: 4.0–5.3 epochs up to 20 tokens per parameter, 6.9–8.3 at 100, and 10.0–11.8 at 300.

With unique data fixed, compute goes to model size and epochs in roughly equal shares, holding τ = D/N at 16–24 until loss stops improving near Rc:

Nopt = √(C/6τ),  Ropt = √(τC/6) / U(5)

Fitted exponents are N ∝ C0.52–0.53 and R ∝ C0.47–0.48. In the fixed-corpus sweeps from 14M to 2B, the loss-minimizing epoch count is R* ≈ 4.5 (U/N)0.51 (R²log = 0.94), so a fixed corpus shifts the optimum to fewer epochs as models grow.

    A second epoch is nearly free; the fourth is not

    Across the main grid, a second epoch is worth 0.87–1.02 fresh tokens at every budget. By four epochs the value falls, and it falls fastest for the largest models.

    Counts are not the whole story

    At identical token counts, how the repeats are laid out still changes loss.

    • Order. Replaying shards consecutively instead of spacing them across epochs costs +0.46 bpb at 511M and 16 epochs. Reshuffling every epoch changes loss by less than 0.01.
    • Allocation. Concentrating repeats on fewer samples, a third of the data four times rather than half of it twice, costs +0.0039 bpb (95% CI [+0.0033, +0.0045]), positive in all 18 paired differences.
    • Source. A source’s token entropy correlates with its loss rise at 16 epochs (r = −0.89). GitHub code rises 0.16 bpb above its minimum; Books does not rise.
    • Re-tokenization. BPE-dropout (p = 0.05) on repeats lowers loss by 0.154 bpb at 16 epochs, but costs 0.021 at one epoch and 0.010 at four, so it pays only under heavy repetition.2

    What this means for a training run

    1. Transfer epoch counts with data, not size. With unique data fixed, larger models should take fewer epochs; when unique data grow with the model, the best epoch count barely changes. To reuse an epoch count on a larger model, scale the unique data with it.
    2. Larger budgets per parameter tolerate more repetition. Repeats fall to half the value of fresh tokens after about four to five epochs up to 20 tokens per parameter, and about ten at 300.
    3. Grow model and epochs together, up to Rc. With unique data fixed, extra compute goes to both in roughly equal shares until loss stops improving near the critical epoch count. Ignoring the cost of repetition loses at most 0.002 bpb up to that point.
    4. Spread and shuffle repeats. Space repeats across epochs and samples, repeat lower-entropy sources less, and re-tokenize only under heavy repetition. Compare such choices at separately annealed horizons; intermediate checkpoints can misrank them.

    How we measured

    Grid
    Five sizes from 23M to 361M non-embedding parameters; 5–300 tokens per parameter; 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 epochs with U = D/R; 175 runs
    Sweeps
    Fixed corpus (14M–2B, up to 32 epochs), U = 2.5N (23M–1.2B), D = 20N partial repetition, order, source entropy and re-tokenization studies
    Models
    Decoder-only Transformers with grouped-query attention, RoPE, ReLU² MLPs and tied embeddings; context 1,024
    Optimizer
    Muon for hidden matrices, Adam elsewhere; no weight decay, dropout or clipping; learning rate annealed to each run’s horizon
    Data
    SlimPajama: CommonCrawl, C4, GitHub, Books, arXiv, Wikipedia and StackExchange; GPT-2 byte-level BPE
    Metric
    Bits per byte on held-out test sets of each source; paired studies use three or more replicates
    Compute
    Eight H800 GPUs

    The models are far smaller than frontier systems, and since weight decay lowers the cost of repetition, the constants of Eqs. 3 and 4 may shift under other regularization.
